## 3) Per-family deep dives

### F1 — Cross-sectional momentum (equity)

- **Signal logic.** Rank a universe of ~500+ US equities by cumulative return over a formation window (typically 6–12 months). Buy the top decile/quintile ("winners"), short the bottom ("losers"), hold for 3–12 months; rebalance monthly; conventional implementations skip the most recent week because of the documented 1-month reversal overlap. "6/6" = 6-month formation, 6-month holding.
- **Canonical parameter defaults**: J6/6 (formation 6m, hold 6m, one-week skip) is the canonical Jegadeesh–Titman design. Strategies with 12m formation/3m hold generated the largest raw returns (1.31–1.49%/mo in the paper). Fama–French UMD factor = 2–12 month winner-minus loser. Practical portfolios: monthly rebalance, top/bottom deciles, small-cap tercile skip, exclude financials optional.
- **Papers/authors**: Jegadeesh & Titman, "Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers," JF 1993 (https://www.bauer.uh.edu/rsusme/phd/jegadeesh-titman93.pdf ; https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb04702.x). Fama–French UMD documentation; also Jegadeesh & Titman (1993) hotter post-1990s replicability conversations e.g., Israel–Moskowitz (2013) on after-cost decay.
- **Performance claims**: 6/6 strategy realized 12.01%/yr compounded excess return, 1965–2000 sample; 12m/3m ~1.3–1.5%/mo. Caveat: returns are pre-transaction-cost; academic follow-ups (e.g., Barroso & Santa-Clara 2015 drawdown-scaled; 1998–2013 report post-cost decay to ~half) — treat 12% as an upper bound.
- **Data needed**: daily adjusted OHLCV for 500–3,000 US equities, 25+ years OR CRSP-equivalent. Free tier: yfinance daily data for ~500 large caps is feasible; 25y history ok.
- **Risk profile**: crash risk in 2009/2020-style reversals; December distortion; small-cap shorts expensive; long/short construction in a small account is limited by short availability and PDT rules.
- **$100 free-tier vectorbt?** Yes for backtesting the long leg (top-decile long-only), with a caveat that the short leg is not free exportable in retail; crypto cross-section version needs no short constraint (F15).
- **Public implementation references**: QuantConnect "Stocks on the Move" forum reimplementation (https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/16578/); KobAmoah/Idiosyncratic-Momentum on GitHub (https://github.com/KobAmoah/Idiosyncratic-Momentum).

### F2 — Time-series momentum (TSMOM)

- **Signal logic**: compute the instrument's excess return over the past 12 months; hold long if positive, short if negative; position size inversely proportional to recent volatility (target 40% per-instrument annualized vol, 10% portfolio vol). Rebalance monthly.
- **Canonical parameters**: 12-month lookback, 1-month holding; vol target 30–40%/instrument ex-ante, 10–15% portfolio; monthly rebalance; all 58 instruments equal-weight.
- **Papers**: Moskowitz, Ooi, Pedersen, "Time Series Momentum," JFE 2012 (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X11002613 ; NYU PDF at https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/facdir/lpederse/papers/TimeSeriesMomentum.pdf). AQR "Demystifying Managed Futures" (https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Journal-Article/)
- **Performance claims**: diversified TSMOM gross Sharpe ≈1.8 (AQR 2014, 1985–2012 data); after typical 2/20 fees + transaction costs ≈1.0. Per-MOP: positive in every one of the 58 instruments studied (1985–2009). Caveat: gross of costs, institutional futures; commodity roll yield complicates crypto translation.
- **Data**: daily/monthly futures or ETF data; for backtest on free tier, FX/commodity futures via yfinance ETFs (or futures chains) or crypto perps; no payment required for historical.
- **Risk profile**: draws down hard during systematic reversals (2014, 2020 oil crash, 2022 bond destroyer); long-only alternatives exist.
- **vectorbt-feasible**: Yes. Signal reduces to `sign(rolling_return)`; vol targeting vectorized.
- **Implementations**: AQR's published strategy script lives in the paper; QuantConnect forums have Unix code; `vbt` tutorial pages implement `sign()` signals in literally 20 lines.

### F3 — 52-week-high momentum (anchor-based)

- **Signal logic**: short stocks whose price is far below their 52-week high; long those trading at/near their 52-week high. Anchoring theory: traders resist buying new highs, so prices near the high continue up; prices far below the high continue down.
- **Canonical params**: 52-week window; monthly rebalance; rank by proximity ratio = price / trailing 52-week high.
- **Papers**: George & Hwang, "The 52-Week High and Momentum Investing," JF 2004 (PDF: https://www.bauer.uh.edu/tgeorge/papers/gh4-paper.pdf; journal DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2004.00695.x).
- **Claims**: nearness to 52-week high dominates past-return momentum in predictive regressions, and (unlike JT winners) 52-week winners do NOT reverse over the following 2 years. Noted caveat: monthly-frequency tests, CRSP, pre-cost.
- **Data**: daily OHLCV for ~500-3000 stocks, 20+ years. Free Yahoo data ok.
- **Risk**: crowding; needs short availability; small-cap distribution.
- **vectorbt-free-tier**: Yes, as long-only bucket or long/short proxy.
- **Implementations**: several public GitHub implementations exist; none verified by me this session. `[NOT VERIFIED]`.

### F4 — Clowens-style regression slope momentum ("Stocks on the Move")

- **Signal logic**: score each S&P500 stock by the annualized exponential-regression slope of its log price over ~90 days, multiplied by the regression R² (a volatility/pro-of-trend adjustment); buy the top-ranked ~100 (or 20%), exclude gap-movers >15% over 90 days, and exit names below a 100-day MA; only buy when S&P500 > 200-day MA. Weekly rebalance.
- **Canonical params**: slope window 90d, weekly rebalance, top-100/20% size cap, ATR(20)-based 0.1% risk position sizing — the two most important are in the 2014 book (A. Clenow, *Stocks on the Move*, 2014).
- **Publications**: original book; forum reimplementation post with backtests (QuantConnect, Nov 2024: https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/16578/). Backtest 2009–2024: CAR 8.79% (annualized), MDD 24.1%, win-rate 59%; author's own beat of B&H S&P is modest.
- **Data**: S&P500 daily OHLCV — free.
- **Risk**: winner concentration (few megacaps dominate), regime filter critical, 0.1% risk sizing implies high aspiration on small accounts.
- **vectorbt feasibility**: Yes (daily compute trivial). Note the 200-day regime filter is easy to add.
- **Implementations**: QuantConnect forum post includes full C#/Python code.

### F5 — Intraday market momentum (first half-hour → last half-hour)

- **Signal logic**: compute S&P/ETF index return from prior close to ~10:30 ET (first half hour → last); if positive, buy at ~15:30 and sell ~16:00, or size into that residual correlation day. The predictability is strongest on high-vol/high-volume/recession/news days.
- **Canonical params**: 30-minute bars; signals conditioned on the first half-hour return; hold the last half hour each day; also usable with the 12th half-hour return as extra predictor.
- **Paper**: Gao, Han, Li, Zhou, "Market Intraday Momentum," JFE 2018 (ScienceDirect; SSRN 2552752).
- **Claims**: R²_in-sample 1.6%, OOS R² 1.4%; economically significant timing; strong in 11+ active ETFs 1993–2013. Caveat: intraday costs/latency — a retail day-trader at typical spreads likely nets much of it after an optimized, tested filter; the reported numbers are gross.
- **Data**: 1-min or 30-min bars (free: yfinance intraday history on SPY is daily-file-limited (~730 days; real 1-min history via free APIs is shallow) — **Y with limited history**; crypto-index intraday proxies have long free history.
- **Risk**: execution timeliness; huge tail in 2008/2020; not a "sleep at night" edge.
- **vectorbt feasibility**: technically yes on 1h/30m bars; investor-friendly caveat about data depth.

### F6 — Short-term reversal (weekly/monthly)

- **Signal logic**: at trading frequency (week or month), buy stocks that performed worst, sell those that performed best; hold one period; monthly version forms deciles on prior-month returns.
- **Evidence**: Jegadeesh (1990), "Evidence of Predictable Behavior of Security Returns," JF 1990 (DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.1990.tb05110.x): monthly frequency — extreme-decile spread 2.49%/mo, 1934–1987 (pre-cost); Da, Liu, Schaumburg (2011), "Decomposing Short-Term Return Reversal," NY Fed Staff Report 513: standard reversal α 0.33%/mo (insign.), within-industry reversal 1.20%/mo (t=5.9), DR-news-based 1.34%/mo (t=9.3).
- **Caveat**: classical literature averages aggressively over a very stale period; the modern alpha is concentrated in liquidity-shock names; cost erosion is brutal for retail.
- **Data**: daily closes for universe; vectorization easy.
- **vectorbt**: yes but expect fails after realistic costs; OPINION: best avoided as primary family.
- **Implementations**: Da–Liu–Schaumburg methodology is public; no canonical repo `[NOT FOUND]`.

### F7 — Pairs trading: standard distance method (Gatev et al.)

- **Signal logic**: over a 12-month formation period, find the pair of stocks with the minimum sum of squared differences between normalized log-price series; trade when the normalized spread deviates > 2σ from its formation mean; exit when it returns to mean (0σ).
- **Canonical params**: formation 12 months, trading 6 months; top 20 pairs; 2σ entry / 0σ exit; long-short in $1 equal-weight notional (1:1); optional same-industry restriction.
- **Papers**: Gatev, Goetzmann, Rouwenhorst, "Pairs Trading: Performance of a Relative-Value Arbitrage Rule," RFS 2006 (https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v19y2006i3p797-827.html). Update: Do & Faff 2010/2012 (CRSP net-of-cost declines; top-4 MVPK portfolios 3.4%/yr in 1962–2009; profitability concentrates in crisis regimes such as 2000–2002).
- **Claims**: up to 11% annualized for top-20 (pre-cost); 91 bps/mo pre-cost, 38 bps/mo post-cost in the Rad–Low–Faff larger update (1962–2014) — post-2002 decay in trade frequency.
- **Data**: 100+ daily price series per universe (2-3k needed for top-20 top-decile confidence); equities via yfinance, crypto via ccxt.
- **Risk**: correlation shock (single pair divergence → open loss), need for short, counterparty (crypto futures).
- **vectorbt**: Yes; GGR-jupyter implementation inside repo: polkarowo/vectorbt `examples/PairsTrading.ipynb` (https://github.com/polkarowo/vectorbt/blob/master/examples/).

### F8 — Pairs trading via cointegration (Engle–Granger / Johansen)

- **Signal logic**: estimate cointegration regression/log-price, compute residual spread, z-score, open long-short at ±2σ, close at ±0.5 or mean; optionally add a half-life (OU) filter on the residual. (Johansen for multivariate portfolios; ADF for pair-9.)
- **Current practice (2023–2026)**: pre-selection of pairs matters enormously; log-price correlation pre-select beats SSD/covariance (Rad et al 2010; Yip 2023, Statistical Papers — pre-selection paper). (2024) asymptotic mean + convergence-rate filter improves Sharpe on Taiwan ETF constituents (Shi et al., Computational Economics). (2025) ETF pair study 2000–2024: lower z-threshold → more trades, more profit BUT higher DD; cointegration stability is the success bound.
- **Canonical**: 12m formation, 6m trading, z-enter 2.0, z-exit 0–0.5; some use z-enter 2.5/exit 0 with 5% stop.
- **Papers**: classic EG 1987 & Johansen; Rad–Low–Faff 2016 (85/43 bps/mo); GFZ overview (Radlow github paper includes framework).
- **Data**: daily closes; statsmodels for ADF/Johansen can run inside vectorbt's `Indicator.from_signals` precompute; the [morph] speed fine for ≤2000 pairs.
- **Risk**: beta drift/cointegration breaks (whale slippage in crypto); size pairs by dollar, not σ.
- **vectorbt-feasible**: Yes — a canonical notebook exists (see F7). Efficiency is fine at daily.

### F9 — Statistical arbitrage by s-score (Avellaneda–Lee)

- **Signal logic**: for each stock, regress its return on K sector/factor ETF returns; estimate residual process X_t = log(S_t) − β·log(ETF_t) − α; define s-score = normalize residual by standard deviation; enter long if s < −1.25, short if s > +1.25, exit long when s > −0.5 (long-exit 0.5), exit short when s < +0.75.
- **Canonical params**: multi-factor (2–8 ETFs including S&P, sector, size), 60-day estimation window ending daily; entry 1.25, exit long 0.50 / short 0.75.
- **Paper**: Avellaneda & Lee, "Statistical Arbitrage in the U.S. Equities Market," Quantitative Finance 2010 (https://math.nyu.edu/~avellane/AvellanedaLeeStatArb2007).
- **Claims**: mean 0.7%/mo alpha gross of costs for 4-factor model, 1992–2007 in-sample; significant decay post-2010 (they note regime change), i.e., the edge is real but busy.
- **Data**: 100–1000 daily prices + factor ETFs; 10+ yrs.
- **Risk**: model drift / regime; capital spread over sector exposes to beta-neutral residual; risk realized only if factor betas stable.
- **vectorbt**: Yes — pipeline = OLS per stock per date (use numpy timed); the published cutoffs are directly transferable.

### F10 — Channel breakout («Turtle» / Donchian)

- **Signal logic**: enter long when price breaks prior 20-day high (System-1) or 55-day high (System-2); exit on opposite-side short 10-day (S1) or 20-day (S2) low; add units at ½N intervals; stop at 2N; size units = 1% equity per 2N move (2% risk).
- **Canonical params**: N = TrueRange 20-day MA (i.e., ATR-20); System widths above; max 4 units per market; pivot filters: S1 skips the first 20-day breakout of a 55-day range that previously failed; position-weight cap at 10 units per axis.
- **Papers/source**: Curtis Faith, *Way of the Turtle*; original rules PDF: https://tradingwithrayner.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/OriginalTurtleRules.pdf (Curtis M. Faith). Academic support: DMA/channel parameter sweep in commodity futures — Szakmary, Shen, Sharma 2010 (J. Banking & Finance) — 48 years, 28 markets, all schemes positive net of costs in ≥22/28.
- **Performance claims**: Turtles themselves report avg 80%/yr over the original 1983–87 span — candidly noted as legendary-select benchmark; far from vectorized replications.
- **Data**: daily OHLC 10+ instruments at least (futures or crypto perps).
- **Risk**: whipsaw in regimes without trends; stop/position logic cheap when vectorized.
- **vectorbt-free-tier**: Yes — the whole unit math: vectorized with `vbt.Signal`… existing notebook: `polakowo/vectorbt/examples` has breakout-style signals; Turtle spec is easy to transcription.
- **Implementations**: turtle papers exist; QuantConnect bootcamps pattern (TurtleBooleans public repo on LEAN forums; verified link: QuantConnect forum Bo & "Boot Camp" thread). `[PARTIAL: no direct repo link captured for turtle specifically (only original PDF)]`.

### F11 — Dual moving-average crossover

- **Logic**: long when short MA(1) > long MA(150/200), short/cash otherwise; optional 1% band to filter whipsaw.
- **Canonical**: (1,200), (1,150), (5,150), (2,200) + 1% bands; variable-length (VMA) vs fixed-length (FMA) with 10-day holds.
- **Evidence**: Brock, Lakonishok, LeBaron 1992 (JF) — DJIA daily 1897–1986: VMA (1,,0) buy-day mean +0.042%/day (~12%/yr), sell-day −0.025%/day (~−6%); bootstrap-valid; their SEL interpretation: buy signals predict periods; not efficient-market-clear (challenged by later studies on data-snooping (Sullivan–Timmermann–Frank 1999 suggest survival of best-of-universe rules; for callable to be cautious). Additional commodity evidence: Szakmary 2010.
- **Data**: daily OHLC, 20+ yrs; crypto-friendly.
- **Risk**: whipsaw; long-only vs long/short differences; transaction costs on sell-half.
- **vectorbt feasibility**: yes — trivial.
- **Implementations**: PyQuantbot? They cover breakout/сellar not MA; QuantConnect Boot Camp thread covers MA momentum (`forum/discussion/12929`).
- **Note**: `[jerne]` the FD data for the search found FMA test fine; not a criticism.

### F12 — Volatility breakout / EMA+ATR / Keltner

- **Logic**: long when close > EMA + m×ATR (volatility-scaled breakout); exit at close < EMA (or trailing channel); optional ADX filter (> threshold filter weak trends); short symmetric.
- **Canonical params** (public sweep): EMA 10–80 (step 5), ATR 10/14/20/28, multiplier 0.5–2.0, ADX window 10/14/20/28, entry ADX ≥ 20–30, exit ADX < 15–25. Keltner variant: EMA 20–50, ATR 7/14/21, mult 0.5–2.5.
- **Evidence base**: the parameter conventions are community-styled (PyQuantLab Medium series, 2025–26: https://pyquantlab.medium.com/parameter-optimization-for-a-breakout-strategy-in-vectorbt-ca4161eff245, and rolling variant for Keltner: 2025 post). No formal peer-reviewed evidence for the EMA+ATR combo specifically — `[No rigorous academic source located; treat as engineering pattern]`.
- **Data**: daily OHLC; fine.
- **Risks**: many tradable configs; grid over 6k+ combos — serious PBO (see F21); cost-sensitive.
- **vectorbt**: Yes — the exact example from the tutorial is effectively a free-tier vectorbt exercise; and includes fees/slippage 0.10%/0.05% and a `vbt.Portfolio.from_signals` pattern.

### F13 — Bollinger / RSI classic mean reversion (single-instrument)

- **Logic**: long when close < lower Bollinger(20, 2σ) or RSI(14) ≤ 30; exit at mid-band/RSI 50; mirror short. (No consensus on strongest parameter set; typical retail defaults: 20/2; RSI 14.)
- **Evidence literature**: no major academic paper directly proves Bollinger edge; the broad short-term reversal literature (F6) is the closest support; regime (trend) dependency is the known killer. Mean-reversion backtest guides (e.g., backtrex, Jul-2026 article https://backtrex.com/en/blog/mean-reversion-strategy-backtesting-guide) document that performance collapses in trending regimes and that entry z thresholds are highly sensitive.
- **Parameters**: z 2.0/1.8; RSI 30/70; exit z 0; stop optional 1 ATR.
- **vectorbt**: Yes.
- **Risk**: brutal in trend; cost sensitive; position concentration single-instrument.
- **Note**: `[NOT FOUND]` peer-reviewed treatment — keep as engineering/retail staple with caveats.

### F14 — Bitcoin hour-of-day (time-of-day) seasonality

- **Signal logic**: hold BTC (long-only) only during historically strong UTC hours (21:00–23:00 UTC), flat otherwise; optional regime filter price > SMA-200d.
- **Evidence**: Baur, Cahill, Godfrey, Liu (Finance Research Letters 2019 — BTC time-of-day/day-of-week/month effects, 15M obs, 7 exchanges: statistically time-specific return anomalies (relief), persistent volume effects, evening volume dip etc.). Community quant backtest (QuantifiedStrategies article [2026]): two best hours 21–23 UTC, ~0.07%/hr average over studied window; Monday best day; SMADD filter improved the strategy modestly.
- **Caveats**: the same paper stresses non-persistence of return anomalies across periods — i.e., window-sensitive; do NOT over-trust decade-average numbers.
- **Parameters**: window 2h daily, optional SMA-200 filter, 100% allocation.
- **vectorbt**: Y (1h bars free).
- **Implementations**: the strategy files are in the article; no standalone repo captured `[NOT FOUND]`.

### F17pr — Crypto cross-section momentum 1–4 weeks (Liu–Tsyvinski–Wu)

- **Logic**: weekly quintile sorts on 1-, 2-, 3-, 4-week past returns; long top quintile, short bottom (or long-only ranked top 20); rebalanced weekly; coin universe with free public daily+ data (1,707 coins in paper).
- **Evidence**: Liu, Tsyvinski, Wu (2022, 3-factor crypto model; NBER w25882): momentum L/S ≈ 3%/wk for 1–4w lookbacks (higher for 3w), significant; long-run (8w+) momentum insignificant; it survives a 3-factor model (CMKT, CSMB, CMOM). Caveat: short leg includes many small coins — capacity/borrow constraints; 3-month test windows; tiny-cap filter needed.
- **Parameters**: weekly rebalance; lookback 3w default; quintiles; skip=0.
- **vectorbt**: Yes (weekly aggregation; yfinance coin = daily).
- **Risk**: extreme skew; illiquidity discounts in the short side.

### F16 — Funding-rate harvesting (delta-neutral)

- **Logic**: when perp funding > threshold, long spot perp: short the same notional on the perp; collect 8h funding payments; soften basis change. Stop during extended backdated funding.
- **Presentations**: He, Manela, Ross, von Wachter (2023, `"Fundamentals of Perpetual Futures"` arXiv:2212.06888) — no-arb bounds + theoretical basis & margin; empirical: fund-rate arb high Sharpe ≈2.00 for BTC across 2019–21; deviations comove; 2/3 of profit from basis convergence, 1/3 from funding when regime tight. Bagtester guide (https://bagtester.com/guides/funding-rate-arbitrage): realistic 2022–24 BTC; funding capture 8–15%/yr gross, Sharpe 1.0–1.8 "quiet" regimes, negative in basis squeezes; keel.io funding-carry (2024–2026 Hyperfluid perp cross-section, shorting high-funding names): Sharpe 1.69, +58.8% total, −12.8% MDD net of fees in $ h sample — `OPINION: vendor backtests; treat as in-sample evidence`.
- **Params**: threshold 1 bps per 8h (≈5% annualized); full delta-neutral; rebalance 8 hourly; close if funding ≤ threshold.
- **Data**: funding-rate history (Binance public API / ccxt exposes `fundingRate`), perp+spot OHLC.
- **vectorbt**: Yes — needs custom funding fixture, but cheap; backtest feasible on free tier.
- **Risks**: regime flip, funding negative, basis vs spot divergence, exchange/dirty counterparty — the "convergence half" is not frictionless.
- **Implementations**: Bagtester guide template; `-reference` link documented in source; Keel strategy page declares exact Hyperfluid data and settings (public).

### F17 — Perp–spot basis arbitrage / "basis trade"

- **Logic**: if perp trades above spot by > transaction-cost band, long spot / short perp (or reverse when below); unwind on convergence; funding receipts as part of the carry. One-sided reserves on both legs.
- **Params**: entry basis ≥ borrow-rate + fees (approx per-perp annualize); positions per 8h funding schedule; delever on basis squeeze beyond stop (liquidation).
- **Paper**: He–Manela–Ross–von Wachter 2022 (theory); wicker 2025 "Fundamentals" numeric claims: Sharpe 2.0 BTC; he also studies CEX/DEX (ApolloX, Drift) 2025 (ScienceDirect) — reports up to 115.9% per six months with 1.92% low loss — `OPINION: implausibly smooth; likely curated windows; not independently replicated`.
- **vectorbt scope**: backtest with synthetic short-leg fees realistic; crude if ignoring margins.
- **Risks**: sub-liquid basis for altcoins; liquidation on perp leg; funding gap to spot.

### F18 — Grid trading (static and dynamic)

- **Logic**: place buy/sell per grid-level at intervals below/below current price; "sell at upper grid, buy at lower grid", collecting spread on each crossing; redefine grid to present price when breaking boundaries.
- **Canonical**: geometric grid (ratio), e.g., 1.5–3%, half-grid count 20–50 either side; e.g., DGT (higher dynamic) resets the grid at new center and re-invests profits.
- **Evidence**: (i) academic-like paper (arXiv:2410.11921, DGT 2024): shows static grid EV ≈ 0 before costs; dynamic- grid DGT (2021–2024 BTC/ETH, 1-min candles) IRR 60–70% and MDD vs sell/hold 2021−2024 — BUT could be regime (trading range + up) and yearly MDD vs B&H; the counterpoint (Stevens/FE800 2024 study) found ML-adjusted grids did not outperform plain grids, and that). 
- **vectorbt**: yes; for fee sensitivity — drop records on fee.
- **Risk**: silent fee drag (每 tick), zero-EV baseline without costs (paper-backed), trend-out-of-grid exposures; DGT expected drawdown series less than single hold in the tested period, not guaranteed.
- **Implementations**: kpaulsen97/dynamic-grid-trading repo (https://github.com/kpaulsen97/dynamic-grid-trading) with cross-validation tooling.

### F19 — BTC lump-sum vs DCA conditional on power-law multiple

- **Logic**: entry valuation = price / power-law trend (Santostasi fit). Below ~1.25× trend, deploy lump sum; above, DCA over 12 mo; the threshold ≈ crossover 50% win in historical windows (14,583 entry-date sims; BTC 2010–2026).
- **Evidence claim**: below 0.75× trend LS wins 95–100% at 1–4y horizons; above 2.7× DCA wins 93%+ (btcpowerlaw.nl / Scale-1.pdf). Vanguard (2012) equity lump-sum ≈67%; — crypto regime adjustment. OPPOSITE/PROPER: this is a capital-deployment rule, not a trading signal, and there's **OPINION**: fits small-account context well (no periodic trading fees, no direction risk beyond BTC itself).
- **vectorbt**: trivial (daily BTC price).
- **Risks**: power-law model itself (time-varying floor); backtest accepts its assumptions; not verified by me beyond the report.

### F20 — Cross-exchange (venue) arbitrage

- **Logic**: buy on low-venue, sell on high-venue identical asset (BTC across CEXs or BTC/USDT pairs; sometimes tri-arb); the GoTwitter funds flow risk; hold reserves on both venues (capital-intensive, efficiency with withdrawal asset on-chain confirm; settlement latency risky).
- **Evidence**: (2025) Guo et al., Finance Res FEAS "bitcoin arbitrage and exchange default risk" 16 exchanges, 2013–2026; arb windows persist when riskier exchanges price higher; faster when liquid; slower when fee conquestion. Makarov–Schoar (2020) 64% avg price gaps; 2024 theses (Vaasa) replicate persistence; 80-exchange paper (SSRN 2024) 2019–2023: BTC arbitrage spread ranges 8.7–15.7% cross-exchange periods (high on non-US/untrusted venues; DEXs higher than CEX).
- **Params**: spread threshold ≥ (transfer fee + x), size; each venue 50% capital reserve; fresh bitcoin moved only on confirmation.
- **vectorbt backtest**: feasible using public daily/hourly API data for price differences AND historical fees; but execution edge is dominated by latency/faust — vectorbt line-out NOT match real walk.
- **Risks**: dry/так exchange default (FTX), settlement latency, network congestion — the paper says these are the frictions; no free lunch.

### F21 — Meta: backtest-overfitting hygiene (PBO/DSR, walk-forward)

- **Logic**: not a trade signal; a gating procedure: compute probability of backtest overfitting (Bailey–Borwein–López de Prado–Shakery PBO), deflated Sharpe ratio (DSR) with candidate counts; and use walk-forward (WFO) / combinatorial CV; reject strategies whose best params are 1-σ spikes; report all candidates not just the winner.
- **Evidence**: Keel "Overfitting in Crypto Backtests" (2026) — PBO can exceed 50% on as few as 100 trials; DSR/PBO flows; the deep-RL paper list shows test-on-2022 inseparable issues (arXiv:2209.05559 [crypto DRL overfit detection], released test period: 05-27 Jun 2022, it rejects overfit RL agents). Academic anchors: Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, Zhu (2014) — cited via Keel.
- **Recommends**: keep ANY grid-search-based family (F12, F18) behind PBO/DSR; pre-declare candidate set; cost the backtest; walk-forward.
- **vectorbt**: yes — vectorbt computes thousands of parameter sets at once → reuse the same matrix to compute PBO directly.

## F22 — (advisory, evidentiary) Small-account retail viability

Purpose: frame expectations for a <$10k engine.

- **Factbase**: Chague, De-Losso, Giovannetti, "Day trading for a living?" (2020; SSRN 3423101; PDF at ebiCapital) — Brazil mini-Ibovespa 2012–17: of 19,646 new day traders: 97% of those persisting >300 days lost; only 1.1% earned > min wage; 0.5% > bank-teller salary; no learning curve (probability of profit decreases in days traded).
- Day-trader prevalence in Taiwan 1992–2006 (Barber–Lee–Liu–Odean RFS 2017/2019): <3% consistently profitable day traders; most heavy retail turnover, top-1000 novices.
- **Regulation**: FINRA PDT rule — accounts < $25k cannot day-trade equities/options beyond 4 round-trips per 5 days → severe constraint for small equity strategies (America). Crypto (24/7, no PDT) is the structural escape.
- **Implication for this list**: long-only crypto/ETF variants of F1–F5/F14 (no short constraint), and funding/basis backtests are the genuinely testable set; swing-level turnover is affordable; avoid high-fee micro- strategies.
- **Recommendation (OPINION)**: size expected pass-through costs: 0.1% fees×2 per rotation → only families with ≥15–20% annual gross edge scrutiny; anything that needs >10 trades/day is disqualified.
